Capital Preservation

Risk Management & Position Sizing for New Investors.

Adarsh Chandra, CAO
Nov 25, 2024 8 Min Read

Rule number one of investing: Never lose capital. Rule number two: Never forget rule number one. While Warren Buffett’s famous aphorism sounds simple, executing risk control requires mathematical precision, rigorous sizing formulas, and unemotional asset diversification.

1. The Ruin Problem & Recovery Math

If an asset declines 50%, you do not need a 50% return to get back to even—you need a 100% gain. If an asset plunges 80%, you need an astronomical 400% gain simply to restore your original capital.

This asymmetric recovery penalty is why preserving principal during market drawdowns matters infinitely more than catching every speculative 10x breakout.

2. The 5% Single-Stock Allocation Limit

Except for low-cost broad index funds, retail investors should rarely allocate more than 5% to 8% of total investable liquid capital to any single individual equity, regardless of how strong the conviction feels.

A single regulatory change, accounting anomaly, or technological disruption can impair even blue-chip stalwarts. With an 8% cap, a 50% plunge reduces your total portfolio by only 4%—a bump you can easily recover from.
