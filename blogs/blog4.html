Reading Research Reports Without Getting Overwhelmed.

Adarsh Chandra, CAO
Nov 18, 2024 6 Min Read

Opening a 40-page institutional equity research note can feel intimidating. Dense tables, Greek statistical symbols, and financial acronyms often cause retail readers to skip straight to the "Buy / Sell Target" on the front cover. That is a dangerous shortcut. Here is the exact 4-step framework professional analysts use to dissect any report in under 12 minutes.

Step 1: Check the WACC and Terminal Growth Assumptions

Any target price generated by a discounted cash flow (DCF) model is only as credible as the hurdle rate assumption beneath it. Look at the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC). If an analyst uses an unrealistically low discount rate (e.g. 7% in an 8% interest rate climate), the target is artificially inflated.

Always examine the Sensitivity Table: see how much the valuation drops if terminal growth slows by just 1%.

Step 2: Scrutinize the Footnotes on Adjusted EBITDA

Companies love presenting "Adjusted EBITDA" that removes real expenses like stock-based compensation, restructuring fees, and lease write-offs. Look at operating cashflow (CFO) and subtract capital expenditures (Capex). Real Free Cashflow never lies.

Step 3: Read the 'Key Risks' Section First

The most honest section of any institutional report is the regulatory risk disclosure. What happens if raw material input costs spike 15%? What is the client concentration risk? If the top 3 clients represent 65% of revenue, the company possesses fragile pricing power regardless of past growth.
